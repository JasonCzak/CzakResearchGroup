The Czak Research Group in the Department of Physics and Astronomy at James Madison University uses mathematical modeling and computational physics to understand patterns, dynamics, and complex systems. Most of our work centers on the Gray–Scott reaction–diffusion model: we simulate how spots, stripes, waves, and spatiotemporal chaos emerge, and we study what happens when control is applied to only a small region of a chaotic system.

The group is led by [Dr. Jason Czak](https://jasonczak.github.io/), and nearly all of the research is done with undergraduates. Since 2023, students in the group have presented at the APS Global Physics Summit, the Southeastern Section of the APS (SESAPS), and the National Conference on Undergraduate Research.

## What we study

- **Pattern formation:** reaction–diffusion systems, nonlinear dynamics, and the emergence and control of spatial and temporal structures.
- **Scientific computing:** GPU-accelerated simulation, numerical reliability, data analysis, and reproducible research workflows.
- **Complex systems:** spatiotemporal epidemic models, dendritic growth, and computational ecology.
