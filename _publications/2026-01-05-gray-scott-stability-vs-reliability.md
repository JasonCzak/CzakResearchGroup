The Euler method is widely used for numerically solving differential equations because of its simplicity, but a time step that keeps a simulation numerically stable does not guarantee that the computed trajectory is reliable. Using the Gray–Scott reaction–diffusion model, this work examines the accuracy of Euler integration across distinct behavioral regimes and near regime transitions. The threshold for reliable results depends strongly on the dynamics, with increased sensitivity near transitions; chaotic regimes in particular require a drastically smaller time step.

Undergraduate coauthor Owen Jones presented this work at the [92nd Annual Meeting of SESAPS](https://meetings.aps.org/Meeting/SES25/Session/J02.5) (October 2025) and the APS Global Physics Summit (March 2026).
